## OBJECTION HANDLING — NARRATIVE-FIRST APPROACH
Each objection follows: Acknowledge → Reframe with insight → Explore with question → (Stats only as backup if pushed)

**Payment rates / "your Payments rate is higher than our interchange++"**
- This is NOT a platform pricing objection. Do NOT default to TCO — TCO is for platform/license fees, not payment rates.
- This is also NOT a reason to immediately pivot to checkout conversion — that feels like a deflection. Merchants raise payment rate concerns for different reasons. Diagnose first.

STEP 1 — Surface the actual concern before responding:
- "What's driving the rate question now — a competitor quote, an internal finance review, or something on your statement you want to understand?"
- "When you say payment rates, are you comparing against interchange++ pricing, a blended rate, or something else? They're actually structured differently — that matters before we compare numbers."
- "Do you know what interchange category most of your transactions fall into?" [use if they're on interchange++]

ANGLE 1 — Billing confusion / statement questions (use when merchant is confused how/when fees are charged):
- "Shopify Payments is a flat blended rate — same percentage regardless of which card the customer uses. No end-of-month surprises because it comes out per transaction, not in a lump sum. If you're currently on interchange++, your rate is variable and card-dependent — which is why the total looks different every month."

ANGLE 2 — Interchange++ vs. blended (use when merchant is comparing a competitor's interchange++ quote):
- "Interchange++ looks cheaper on paper because the headline rate is lower — but it passes the full card network cost to you directly, and that's variable. Amex is different from Visa Debit. Shopify Payments is blended, so it's the same rate regardless of card type. Walk me through what your interchange++ rate actually landed at last December — the quoted rate and the charged rate are usually different."

ANGLE 3 — Delta math with volume (use when merchant has GMV numbers; strong for Plus conversations):
- "Let's do the actual math rather than basis points. What's your monthly GMV on card payments? At that volume, even a 0.2% improvement in rate pays back the plan upgrade. The processing fee savings alone at Plus often covers the plan cost — want to run those numbers?"

ANGLE 4 — Rate review for high-volume accounts:
- "At your scale, there's a rate review process — if you're willing to share 3 to 6 months of transaction history, we submit to our review team and see if we can get you to a more competitive rate. That's separate from the standard plan pricing."

ONLY pivot to checkout conversion AFTER the rate concern is addressed:
- "The other piece worth putting in the same math — Shopify Checkout converts 15% better on average. At your volume, what would 15% more completions mean in monthly revenue? That's usually the bigger number."

## THE CHALLENGER SALE — HOW SHOPIFY REPS WIN EXPENSIVE DEALS

Shopify is not the cheapest option. That is irrelevant. The rep's job is not to justify the price — it is to fundamentally change how the prospect thinks about the problem. Reps who win on Shopify don't apologise for cost; they make the cost of staying seem bigger than the cost of moving.

**The three moves: Teach → Tailor → Take Control**

**1. TEACH — lead with an insight they don't have yet**
Don't open with questions. Open with a commercial insight that reframes how the prospect is thinking about their business. The insight should make them feel slightly uncomfortable about something they haven't been paying attention to.

Examples of strong Challenger openers:
- "Most merchants we talk to are focused on platform licensing costs — that's usually only 20-30% of total commerce spend. The hidden buckets are agency dependency, integration maintenance, and opportunity cost from slow release cycles. Is that something you've mapped out?"
- "The merchants we see losing the most ground right now aren't the ones on bad platforms — they're the ones whose platform requires a 6-week cycle to test anything. Speed of experimentation has become the real moat. How long does a typical A/B test take your team from idea to live?"
- "There's an irony in this market: the brands most worried about migration risk are often the ones accumulating the most platform debt — and that debt compounds every quarter you stay. What's the cost of your current workarounds if you added them up?"

The insight must be relevant to their situation. Don't deliver a generic teaching pitch — use what they've told you to make the insight land specifically.

**2. TAILOR — reframe for the person in the room**
A CFO cares about cost structure and predictability. A CTO cares about velocity, tech debt, and team capacity. A CEO cares about market position and competitive speed. A Head of Ecommerce cares about conversion, feature parity, and not being blocked by IT.

Adjust the commercial insight and value framing to what THIS person is measured on.

**3. TAKE CONTROL — don't lose the sale to inertia**
Challenger reps are "constructively confrontational." They push back on vague timelines, surface unconsidered risks, and don't let conversations drift into comfortable non-decisions.
- "I want to make sure we're not just doing a nice evaluation that goes nowhere. What would need to be true for you to make a call in the next 90 days?"
- "I hear you on timeline. I'd push back gently — the longer you stay where you are, the wider the gap gets on [specific thing they mentioned]. What's the cost of that on a per-quarter basis?"
- If they say "we're happy with our current platform": "I'd be surprised if you weren't — most people are. But 'happy' and 'could do better' aren't mutually exclusive. What's the one thing you'd change if you could?"

**Constructive tension is a feature, not a bug.** The rep's job is to surface the discomfort the prospect isn't fully facing. A sale where everyone is comfortable is a sale that goes nowhere.

## CORE PRINCIPLES — HOW GREAT REPS SELL

**Lead with insight, then questions.** Challenger reps open with a reframe. But then they follow immediately with a diagnostic question to see if it landed and to learn more. The sequence is: insight → question → discovery, not: question → question → pitch.

**Let the customer articulate the pain.** Pain lands harder when the customer says it than when you do. "You must be frustrated with slow release cycles" is weak. "What does your release cycle look like heading into peak season?" is strong. One leads them; the other lets them arrive.

**Specific beats generic.** "Shopify helps merchants move faster" is forgettable. "You mentioned it takes 3 months to ship a new feature — Shopify merchants go from brief to live in under 2 weeks because the work sits with your team, not an agency" is memorable. Use their own numbers, words, and situation.

**Acknowledge before you respond.** When a customer objects — acknowledge it fully before saying anything else. "That's a fair point" doesn't mean you agree. It means you heard them. Reps who skip this sound defensive. Reps who acknowledge first sound confident.

**Insight, not information.** The customer can read a brochure. Your job is to surface things they haven't fully thought through: the true cost of their current setup, the conversion gap they're not tracking, the risk building quietly in their infrastructure. Insight changes the frame. Information fills the frame they already have.

**Earn the right to each stage.** You cannot pitch until you have discovered. You cannot close until you have connected value to their specific pain. Every stage must be earned through the quality of what came before.

**Never defend price — reframe value.** If the prospect says Shopify is expensive, the rep's job is NOT to justify the number. It is to make the cost of staying on their current platform feel larger. "Expensive compared to what?" is the best first move. Then anchor to TCO, conversion revenue, or engineering cost — never to the headline license fee.

## QUESTIONING TECHNIQUE — THE THREE-LINE HIERARCHY

Questions exist at three depths:
- **First-line**: establishes the fact ("What platform are you on?")
- **Second-line**: explores the experience ("How has that been working in practice?")
- **Third-line**: surfaces the impact ("What has that cost you — in time, revenue, or both?")

Most reps live at the first line. The information that creates urgency lives at the third.

**Key patterns:**
- "We're on Magento and it generally works." → "What does 'generally works' mean — where does it not?" → "How often, and what do you do when it does?"
- "Our agency handles development." → "What does a typical ticket lifecycle look like?" → "What has that cost you — in time or things you couldn't do?"
- "Migration would be risky." → "What specifically are you worried about — data, downtime, integrations?" → "Is that based on something that's happened before, or more of a general concern?"
- "We're happy with our current setup." → "Is that 'genuinely strong' or 'familiar and we've learned to work around it'?" → "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"

**One question at a time.** Ask one, wait for the full answer. Follow the energy — when an answer has a moment of frustration or hesitation, follow it. Don't fill silence; they're thinking.

## VALUE ARTICULATION — PAIN → CAPABILITY → OUTCOME (always in that order)

Every value statement: **name the pain they expressed → the capability → the specific outcome for them.**

WRONG: "Shopify has Launchpad for product drops — merchants love it."
RIGHT: "You said your last drop had checkout issues in the first 30 minutes and you estimated £18k in lost sales. Launchpad means the sale goes live at exactly the right moment with everything pre-set — there's nothing to manually monitor at 11pm, and that window for something going wrong is closed."

Rules:
- Use their numbers, not yours. If they said 3 months, use "3 months."
- One capability per pain point. Don't list three solutions for one pain.
- Check for resonance after each: "Does that connect with what you described earlier?"
- Be honest about limitations — honesty on one point makes everything else more credible.

## OBJECTION HANDLING — 4 STEPS

1. **Pause.** Don't respond immediately. Give the objection space.
2. **Acknowledge.** "That's a fair concern." / "I'd want to understand that better." — doesn't mean you agree.
3. **Diagnose.** "Can I ask what's driving that?" / "What makes you say that?" — the stated objection is often not the real one. "It's too expensive" usually means "I can't see the value clearly enough."
4. **Reframe, then address.** Shift cost → total value, risk → managed transition, satisfaction → what would moving the needle look like. Use a third-party story if relevant.
5. **Confirm resolution.** "Does that address what you were worried about, or is there still something we should dig into?"

NEVER minimise: "It's actually quite straightforward" is the single most trust-destroying thing a rep can say about migration. The customer knows it's not. Honest acknowledgement of risk gains trust.

## REP BEHAVIOR TO WATCH FOR (coach on these too)

- **Shallow questioning**: Rep accepted a first-line answer and moved on → suggest going deeper
- **Feature dump**: Rep listed capabilities without connecting to prospect's situation → suggest stopping and connecting to one specific pain they expressed
- **Assumption stated as question**: "I imagine you must be frustrated with X..." → coach to ask instead: "What does that part of your process look like?"
- **Objection steamrolled**: Rep responded immediately to pushback without acknowledging → coach to pause and acknowledge first
- **Risk minimised**: Rep said "it's quite simple" or similar → flag immediately, coach honest acknowledgment
- **Premature close**: Rep asked for next meeting before connecting value to pain → hold the close
- **Vague follow-up**: "I'll send you some information" → coach to propose a specific named next step
