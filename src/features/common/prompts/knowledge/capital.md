## SHOPIFY CAPITAL — PRODUCT MECHANICS

### How the loan works
- **Fixed cost of borrowing (factor rate)**: Not an interest rate. It's a flat fee on the principal. A 8% factor rate on $100K = $108K total repaid. The cost is the same whether you pay it off in 4 months or 12 — it does NOT compound.
- **Remittance rate**: The daily percentage of all Shopify sales taken for repayment. At 20% remittance, a merchant doing $5K/day repays $1K/day. On slow days, less comes out. On big days, more — but the total is always capped at the fixed fee.
- **Trade-off rule**: Remittance rate and factor rate are inversely linked. If the merchant wants a lower factor rate, they can accept a higher remittance rate (more out daily but cheaper overall). If they want lower daily impact on cash flow, the factor rate goes up slightly. Use this to unstick rate objections.
- **Document threshold**: Offers under $500K require no financial documents. Offers $500K–$999K require income statements, balance sheet, and cash flow statements for FY23–25. Offers $1M+ also require a debt disclosure form (list of current outstanding debts).
- **Funding speed**: 1–4 business days after application approved.
- **Repayment**: Automatic daily deduction from Shopify payouts across ALL payment gateways and channels linked to the store.
- **Renewal rule**: After 51% of the current loan is repaid, a new offer becomes available in admin. The merchant can apply for the second loan but doesn't start repaying it until the first is fully settled — never two loan payments simultaneously.
- **Offer eligibility**: Driven by Shopify sales history, not credit score. Stronger sales history and prior Capital repayment history → higher offers and better rates.
- **Offer presentation**: Capital reps can publish custom manual offers directly to the merchant's Shopify admin, adjusting amount, factor rate, and remittance rate within underwriting guidelines.

### Compliance — MANDATORY at start of every Capital call (all five, in order)
1. Recording consent: "I'm recording this call for notes and training purposes — is that okay with you?"
2. Name confirmation: Each attendee on the call must verbally confirm their full first and last name.
3. Business name confirmation: Ask the merchant to confirm their legal business name.
4. myShopify URL: Ask them to navigate to Settings → Domains and read out the URL ending in .myshopify.com.
5. Authorization: "Are you authorized to speak about Shopify Capital on behalf of the account owner?"

If any of these are skipped early in the call, the Capital rep is non-compliant. Surface a COACH card immediately.

## QUALIFICATION FRAMEWORK FOR CAPITAL CALLS (FUNDS)

Use this instead of MEDDPICC for Capital calls. MEDDPICC elements do not apply.

- **F — Funds Purpose**: What will the capital be used for? (Inventory/stock procurement, seasonal cash flow, marketing spend, new product development, hiring, operational costs). The stronger the business rationale, the easier underwriting goes.
- **U — Urgency**: When do they need the money by? What happens to the business if they don't get it in time? Is there a specific order, contract, or seasonal window driving this?
- **N — Need Amount + Rate Expectations**: What's their target amount? What factor rate and remittance rate would work for them? Do they want to minimize daily cash flow impact (lower remittance) or minimize total cost (lower factor)?
- **D — Decision Authority**: Are they the account owner or authorized to speak about Capital? Who else needs to be involved in the decision?
- **S — Situation (Other Financing)**: What other lending do they have or are evaluating? Bank loan? Wayflyer? Clearco? PayPal Working Capital? Line of credit? This defines where Shopify Capital fits (supplementary vs. primary).

## OBJECTION HANDLING — CAPITAL-SPECIFIC

### "The rate / factor rate is too high"
- DO NOT defend the rate. DO NOT immediately offer a lower rate.
- Diagnose first: "Compared to what? Help me understand what you're looking at so I can give you a proper comparison."
- Reframe the product: "This isn't interest in the traditional sense — it's a fixed fee. If you use the capital well and your business grows, the cost is the same whether you repay in 5 months or 9. The question worth asking: what return can you generate on this capital in that period?"
- Trade-off offer: "We have flexibility here. We can bring the factor rate down if you're comfortable with a slightly higher remittance rate — more comes out daily, but the total cost is lower. Which matters more right now — lower daily cash flow impact, or lower total cost?"
- If they push hard: "Let me run a couple of scenarios with different amounts and show you the trade-off. Would it help if I sent those over after this call?"

### "I have a bank loan / we don't need capital"
- Don't treat this as a lost deal. Reframe as complementary: "That's actually how most of our strongest Capital merchants use us — they keep their bank line for long-term strategic investments and use Shopify Capital for fast, flexible inventory cycles. Bank approvals take 45–60 days. We can have funds in your account in 1–4 days. Are there situations where that speed would matter to you?"

### "We're using Wayflyer / Clearco / PayPal Working Capital"
- Acknowledge: "They're a solid product — makes sense you'd look at them."
- Differentiate: "The difference with us is that repayment happens automatically from your daily Shopify payouts — no separate bank debit, no reconciliation, no invoice to manage. And because our offer is built on your actual Shopify GMV data, the approved amount is often higher than what a third party can see. What amount are they offering you?"
- Position as complementary: "A lot of merchants use both — us for Shopify-native funding and a third party for non-Shopify channels. What does your funding gap actually look like?"

### "The offer amount isn't enough"
- "The offer is driven directly by your Shopify sales history. As your GMV through the platform grows and you build a repayment track record with us, those offers go up — usually meaningfully after the first successful loan. Have you taken Capital with us before?"
- "If you have additional stores on Shopify, those can sometimes be consolidated into a higher offer. How many stores are running through your account?"

### "I need to think about it" / stalling
- Surface the real blocker: "Totally fair. What information would make this easier to decide? And what's your timing — when do you need the funds by?"
- Lock a specific next step: "Let me send you two or three scenarios with different amount and rate combinations tonight. Can we reconnect [specific day] to go through them together?"

### Business structure issues (trusts, partnerships)
- "For trust structures, we lend to the operating company rather than the trust itself. If your business operates as a separate Pty Ltd or Inc. with its own ABN/EIN, we can work with that entity. Is there a company registered separately from the trust?"

## CAPITAL CALL STAGES

1. **Intro / Outreach**: Compliance first. Explain product mechanics. Understand business context (use of funds, amount, urgency). Surface other financing.
2. **Offer Discussion**: Present specific scenarios. Walk through factor rate vs. remittance trade-off. Handle rate objections. Agree on an offer to move forward with.
3. **Underwriting Prep**: Confirm document requirements based on amount. Publish manual offer to admin (requires internal pre-review). Anchor document submission timeline.
4. **Follow-up / Touchbase**: Check on document submission. Re-engage if stalled. Lock a specific decision date.
5. **Renewal**: Merchant has repaid 51%+. Introduce new offer. Discuss growth in offer size. Anchor next draw.
